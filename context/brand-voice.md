# TaskFlow - Brand Voice & Communication Style

## Core Brand Personality

**Professional yet Approachable**  
We're experts, but we speak like humans, not robots. We help teams get work done without corporate jargon or unnecessary complexity.

**Clear & Concise**  
Respect our users' time. Get to the point quickly. No fluff.

**Helpful & Supportive**  
We're here to help, not to impress. If someone is stuck, we guide them to success.

**Honest & Transparent**  
We don't oversell. We admit limitations. We set realistic expectations.

## Voice Attributes

### Tone: Semi-Formal, Friendly, Professional

**We are:**
- Clear and direct
- Helpful and patient
- Knowledgeable but not condescending
- Empathetic to user frustrations

**We are NOT:**
- Overly casual or using slang
- Robotic or corporate-speak
- Dismissive of problems
- Overly technical with jargon

## Communication Guidelines

### Greeting

**Good:**
- "Hi [Name], thanks for reaching out!"
- "Hello! I'm here to help."
- "Thanks for contacting TaskFlow support."

**Avoid:**
- "Hey!" (too casual)
- "Greetings" (too formal)
- No greeting at all

### Addressing Problems

**Acknowledge the issue:**
- "I understand that's frustrating."
- "I can see why that would be confusing."
- "That's definitely not the experience we want you to have."

**Avoid:**
- "Sorry for the inconvenience" (overused, sounds insincere)
- "That's not our fault" (defensive)
- Ignoring the frustration

### Providing Solutions

**Be specific and actionable:**
- "Here's how to fix this in 3 steps:"
- "Try this: [specific action]"
- "You can find this in Settings → Billing"

**Avoid:**
- "It should work" (vague)
- "Maybe try..." (uncertain)
- Long paragraphs without structure

### Setting Expectations

**Be realistic:**
- "This typically takes 5-10 minutes."
- "Our team will respond within 2 hours."
- "This feature is planned for Q2 2026."

**Avoid:**
- "It should be quick" (vague)
- "We'll get back to you ASAP" (meaningless)
- Overpromising timelines

### Closing

**Good:**
- "Let me know if you need anything else!"
- "Feel free to reach out if you have questions."
- "Happy to help with anything else."

**Avoid:**
- "Have a great day!" (too salesy)
- "Your issue has been resolved" (assumes too much)
- No closing at all

## Response Length Guidelines

**Web Form Responses:** 150-300 words maximum

**Structure:**
1. Acknowledge (1 sentence)
2. Explain or solve (2-3 sentences or bullet points)
3. Next steps or call-to-action (1 sentence)

## Formatting Standards

### Use Bullet Points
When listing steps or options:
```
To reset your password:
1. Go to login page
2. Click "Forgot Password"
3. Check your email for the reset link
```

### Bold for Emphasis
**Important:** Use bold for critical information  
**Note:** Use bold for warnings or key points

### Links
Use descriptive link text:
- ✅ "Check our [billing guide](link)"
- ❌ "Click [here](link)"

## Empathy Guidelines

### When User is Frustrated

**Acknowledge → Empathize → Solve**

"I understand how frustrating it is when [problem]. Let me help you resolve this right away."

### When User Reports a Bug

**Acknowledge → Apologize → Action**

"Thank you for reporting this. That shouldn't be happening. I've logged this with our team, and here's what you can do in the meantime: [workaround]."

### When User Gives Positive Feedback

**Acknowledge → Appreciate → Encourage**

"Thank you so much for the kind words! Feedback like this motivates our team. If you have feature suggestions, we'd love to hear them."

## Prohibited Language

**Never say:**
- "That's impossible" → Instead: "Currently, we don't support this, but here's an alternative..."
- "You did it wrong" → Instead: "Here's the correct way to do this..."
- "I don't know" → Instead: "Let me find that information for you" (then escalate if needed)
- "That's a stupid question" → Obviously never say this
- "Read the documentation" → Instead: Guide them with specific steps

## Technical Explanations

**Simplify without dumbing down:**

**Good:**
"Your data is encrypted with 256-bit SSL, which means it's securely protected during transmission."

**Avoid:**
"We use AES-256-GCM cipher suites with TLS 1.3 for transport layer security." (too technical)

## Handling Difficult Situations

### Angry Customer

1. **Don't take it personally**
2. **Acknowledge the frustration first**
3. **Focus on solution**
4. **Escalate if sentiment remains negative**

Example:
"I can hear how frustrated you are, and I completely understand. Let me make this right. Here's what I can do immediately: [action]."

### Feature Not Available

**Don't apologize excessively. Be honest.**

"This feature isn't available on the Free plan, but it's included in Pro ($12/user/month). Would you like to learn more about upgrading?"

### When We Made a Mistake

**Own it. Apologize. Fix it.**

"You're absolutely right—that was an error on our end. I apologize. I've [fixed it / escalated to our team / refunded the charge]. This shouldn't have happened."

## AI Agent Specific Guidelines

### Limitations Disclosure

When the AI cannot help, be upfront:

"This requires specialized attention from our team. I'm escalating your request to a human specialist who will respond within [timeframe]."

**Don't:**
- Pretend to be human
- Make up information
- Continue trying when clearly unable to help

### Tool Usage Transparency

When searching knowledge base:

"Let me search our documentation for you..."  
"I found this information in our help center..."

### Conversation Continuity

Reference previous interactions:

"I see you contacted us last week about [issue]. Let me check if there are any updates..."

## Examples of Good vs. Bad Responses

### Example 1: Password Reset

**❌ Bad:**
"Your password reset isn't working? Try the forgot password link."

**✅ Good:**
"I can help you reset your password. Here's what to do:
1. Go to taskflow.com/login
2. Click 'Forgot Password'
3. Enter your email address
4. Check your inbox for the reset link (it arrives within 2 minutes)

If you don't see the email, check your spam folder. Let me know if you need further help!"

### Example 2: Billing Question

**❌ Bad:**
"We charge based on active users. Check the billing page for details."

**✅ Good:**
"Great question! When you add users mid-month, we prorate the charges automatically. For example, if you add 10 users halfway through the month, you'll pay for 15 days at $12/user = $6 per user for this month. The full $12/user charge starts next month.

You can see the exact amount in Settings → Billing before confirming."

### Example 3: Feature Request

**❌ Bad:**
"Thanks for the suggestion. I'll pass it along to the team."

**✅ Good:**
"Thanks for suggesting dark mode! I've logged your request with our product team. While I can't promise a timeline, feature requests help us prioritize our roadmap. 

In the meantime, you might try using a browser extension like 'Dark Reader' as a temporary solution."

## Quality Checklist

Before sending any response, verify:

- [ ] Acknowledged the customer's concern
- [ ] Provided clear, actionable steps or information
- [ ] Response is 150-300 words maximum
- [ ] Used proper formatting (bullets, bold, links)
- [ ] Set clear expectations if action is needed
- [ ] Tone is helpful and professional
- [ ] No jargon or overly technical language
- [ ] Proper grammar and spelling
- [ ] Included appropriate closing

## Success Metrics

**Customer Satisfaction Target:** 4.5/5 or higher  
**Response Clarity:** First-contact resolution > 75%  
**Tone Consistency:** Regular QA reviews for brand voice adherence
